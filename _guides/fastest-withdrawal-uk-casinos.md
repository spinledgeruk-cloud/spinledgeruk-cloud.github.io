"Fastest withdrawal" lists are one of the most common casino comparison pages online, and one of the least reliable: most are built by ranking marketing claims, not by checking what each operator actually commits to in writing. Here is what we found when we checked, operator by operator, against our own reviews on this site.

## What each operator actually publishes

**Betfair and Paddy Power** (both under PPB Entertainment Limited / Flutter) publish the clearest tiered breakdown of the operators we've reviewed: Pay by Bank withdrawals arrive immediately, an instant card option lands within 1-4 hours, and a standard card option takes 2-5 working days. See our [Betfair review](/reviews/betfair/) and [Paddy Power review](/reviews/paddy-power/) for the full detail.

**LeoVegas** states withdrawals can take "up to five business days" depending on method, without publishing the kind of per-method breakdown Betfair and Paddy Power do. See our [LeoVegas review](/reviews/leovegas/).

**Coral** does not publish a standing withdrawal-time commitment on its help pages at all — it directs you to check your specific method's timeframe inside your account cashier at the time you withdraw. See our [Coral review](/reviews/coral/).

**William Hill** — we could not independently confirm withdrawal times from a primary source for this review, and said so plainly rather than publish a guessed figure. See our [William Hill review](/reviews/william-hill/).

## What this actually tells you

If withdrawal speed specifically is what you're optimising for, Betfair and Paddy Power's Pay by Bank option, with an immediate-arrival commitment backed by a published policy, is the strongest documented claim among the operators covered here — not because the other operators are necessarily slower in practice, but because they publish less detail to check them against. An operator that tells you exactly what to expect, and what to do if it's late, is easier to hold to account than one that says "up to five days" with no method-by-method detail, or one that gives no committed figure at all.

## Why "fastest" claims need one extra layer of scepticism

A stated withdrawal time is a commitment, not a guarantee. Every operator's terms allow for extra time when identity or source-of-funds checks are triggered — this is a Gambling Commission requirement, not something operators choose to add. A first, larger, or unusual withdrawal is more likely to trigger this than a small, routine one, and no operator's headline withdrawal-time figure includes that possibility. If you're choosing an operator specifically because of a fast-withdrawal claim, treat the published figure as what applies once you're a known, verified customer with a clean withdrawal history — not as a guarantee on your very first cash-out.

## If a withdrawal is slower than promised

Raise it with the operator's own complaints process first, in writing, referencing the timeframe they themselves publish. If that goes nowhere, see our [IBAS complaint guide](/guides/ibas-complaint-process-explained/) for what happens if you escalate it further.
