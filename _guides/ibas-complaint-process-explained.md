If a UK-licensed casino refuses to pay out, freezes your account, or rejects a bonus you think you earned fairly, the operator's own complaints process is not the end of the road. Here is what actually happens if you take it further, step by step, without the marketing language.

## What IBAS is

IBAS (the Independent Betting Adjudication Service) is the alternative dispute resolution (ADR) body most UK-licensed gambling operators use to handle unresolved customer complaints. It is independent of any operator, free for players to use, and its decisions are binding on the operator, not on you — if you disagree with the outcome, you can still pursue other routes; the operator cannot.

Not every operator uses IBAS. The Gambling Commission requires every licensed operator to belong to an approved ADR provider, but there are a small number of alternatives. Your operator's terms and conditions, or its complaints page, will name which one it uses.

## Step 1: you have to complain to the operator first

IBAS will not take a case until you have been through the operator's own internal complaints procedure and received a final response, or 8 weeks have passed without one. This step cannot be skipped. Keep records as you go: dates, names of staff you spoke to, exact wording of any decision, and screenshots of anything that might change (a bonus offer, a promotion's terms, your transaction history).

## Step 2: you get a "deadlock letter" or the 8 weeks pass

Once the operator has given you a final decision they will not change, they should issue what is often called a deadlock letter or final response — confirmation that their internal process is exhausted. If they never send one, the 8-week clock from your original complaint is what lets you escalate anyway.

## Step 3: you submit the case to IBAS

This is done through IBAS's own website, not through the operator. You submit your account of events and the evidence you gathered in step 1. IBAS will ask the operator for their side and their own records (account activity, T&Cs in force at the time, communications with you).

## Step 4: IBAS adjudicates

An adjudicator reviews both sides against the operator's terms and conditions as they stood at the relevant time, and against the Gambling Commission's licence conditions and codes of practice. This is the part worth understanding clearly: IBAS decides whether the operator applied its own rules correctly and fairly, not whether those rules were generous. A harsh but clearly stated term, applied consistently, will usually stand. A term that was ambiguous, buried, or applied inconsistently is where complaints tend to succeed.

## What IBAS can and cannot do

IBAS can order an operator to pay out winnings, return a balance, or reverse an account action, when the evidence supports that the operator's own terms were misapplied or unfair. IBAS cannot change an operator's terms and conditions, award compensation beyond the disputed amount, or do anything about a game's odds or RTP — those are not disputes about a specific decision, they are regulatory matters that sit with the Gambling Commission, not with adjudication.

## How long it takes

IBAS does not publish a fixed turnaround, and it varies with case complexity and how quickly the operator responds to information requests. Straightforward cases (a clear-cut bonus term, a documented delay) tend to resolve faster than disputes involving contested account activity or fraud checks. Expect weeks, not days, and plan accordingly if the disputed amount matters to you.

## If you disagree with the outcome

Because the decision only binds the operator, you are not required to accept it. Your options after an unfavourable IBAS decision include the Gambling Commission (for a licence-conditions concern, not a refund), or, for a genuine contractual dispute over a meaningful sum, the small claims court — though a court will look at the same terms and conditions IBAS already reviewed, so a claim built on the same facts IBAS rejected is unlikely to fare differently.

## Why this is worth knowing before you deposit anywhere

The existence of a working ADR process is one of the practical differences between a UKGC-licensed operator and one that accepts UK players without a licence. An unlicensed site has no equivalent obligation to use IBAS or any comparable service, which means a dispute there has no independent adjudicator at all — your only recourse is the operator's own goodwill.
