If you gamble with more than one UK-licensed operator, a new system called "single customer view" is being built to join up what each of them knows about you. It is not live everywhere yet, but the trial is running now, and it will change how self-exclusion and affordability checks work once it rolls out. Here is what it actually is, in plain terms.

## What "single customer view" means

Today, every operator you gamble with — say, LeoVegas and Casumo — holds its own separate picture of your account: your deposits, your losses, your self-exclusion status with that one operator. If you self-exclude from one site, the others do not automatically know.

Single customer view is a shared picture built by linking those separate records together, so that a pattern spread across several operators becomes visible in one place, rather than staying invisible because it is split between accounts.

## Who runs it

The trial is being run by GAMSTOP, the organisation that already operates the UK's national self-exclusion scheme, at the request of the Betting and Gaming Council (the trade body for licensed operators) and with the Gambling Commission's involvement. GAMSTOP was chosen because it already sits outside any single operator and already holds cross-operator self-exclusion data, which makes it the natural place to build a shared view rather than each operator trying to build its own.

## What it tracks

The trial's stated purpose is to give participating operators a combined view of a customer's activity across brands, so they can spot patterns that would not show up by looking at one account alone: for example, a customer who has been asked to slow down or self-exclude by one operator, but keeps opening new accounts elsewhere.

It sits alongside, not instead of, GAMSTOP's existing self-exclusion register: GAMSTOP already blocks you from every UK-licensed site at once when you self-exclude. Single customer view goes further, giving operators shared visibility into patterns of harm even before a player has chosen to self-exclude.

## What it does not do

At the trial stage, single customer view does not automatically close your accounts or set limits for you. It is a data-sharing layer that gives operators more visibility; what each operator does with that visibility (a call from their safer gambling team, a lower deposit limit, a request for an affordability check) is still decided by that operator, under the Gambling Commission's existing rules on customer interaction.

It is also not a credit check or a bank data feed. It draws on gambling activity that operators already hold, not your financial records elsewhere. Affordability checks, which do look at financial data, are a separate and distinct part of the UK's gambling reforms.

## Why it exists

Consultation and industry research have pointed to the same gap: a person showing signs of harm at one operator can simply move to another and look like a fresh, low-risk customer there, because operators historically had no way to see beyond their own front door. Single customer view is meant to close that specific gap.

## What this means for you as a player

If the trial becomes permanent and expands to more operators, expect UK-licensed sites to increasingly act on data from outside your account with them, not just your history with them directly. A pattern that looks fine at one casino in isolation may prompt a conversation or a limit if it looks different across several. This is a shift worth knowing about if you play at more than one licensed operator, even if you have never had a problem: the whole point of the system is to look at the pattern, not the single account.

If you are ever contacted by an operator about your play, or asked to complete an affordability check, that is the framework this trial sits inside. It is separate from, and does not replace, your right to self-exclude directly through [GAMSTOP](https://www.gamstop.co.uk) at any time.
