Most coverage of the UK's new gambling levy is written for operators and lawyers: rate tables, licence categories, compliance deadlines. If you are a player, not a company, here is the same thing explained around the one question that actually matters to you: does this change anything for me?

## What the levy is

Since 6 April 2025, every gambling business licensed by the Gambling Commission has had to pay a statutory levy: a fixed percentage of their gross gambling yield (roughly, what they keep after paying out winnings), collected once a year and passed to the Gambling Commission.

This replaced the system it followed for over a decade, where operators made "voluntary" contributions to fund research, education and treatment for gambling harm, mostly channelled through the industry-funded charity GambleAware. Contributions were not compulsory and varied hugely between operators, so the amount raised depended on individual companies' goodwill rather than a fixed rule.

## Who actually pays it

The levy is paid by the operator, not deducted from your balance or your winnings. It is calculated on the size of the business, not on individual player activity, so there is no line item you will see on your account and nothing for you to pay directly.

Rates are tiered by how a business is licensed and how it operates: online gambling businesses pay a higher percentage than betting shops and other in-person venues, and remote (online) casino and slots operations sit at the top of the scale, reflecting where the Gambling Commission's own research found the highest rates of harm.

## Where the money goes

The Gambling Commission, not the operators, decides how the levy is spent, based on advice from an independent expert committee. The funding is split across three areas:

**Research** into gambling and gambling harm, including exactly how widespread it is and what drives it, run independently of the industry that used to fund it directly.

**Prevention and education**, covering public awareness campaigns and safer gambling messaging, aimed at stopping harm before it starts rather than treating it afterwards.

**Treatment**, funding NHS gambling clinics and other support services people can use if gambling is already causing them problems.

The point of moving from voluntary contributions to a statutory levy, collected by the regulator rather than an industry-run charity, was to remove the operators from deciding how much gets spent on research into their own products and services, and from steering that spending towards work that suited them.

## What actually changes for you as a player

Directly: nothing changes in how you deposit, play or withdraw. No new charge appears on your account, and the levy does not affect the odds, RTP or payout speed of any game.

Indirectly, over time: the research, prevention and treatment funded by the levy is meant to fund better tools and clearer information across the industry, and better-resourced NHS treatment if you or someone you know needs it. Whether that plays out depends on how the money is spent, which is outside any individual player's control, but it is worth knowing where the safer gambling messaging and treatment services you might come across are actually funded from.

## Why this matters for choosing where you play

Every operator licensed by the Gambling Commission pays the levy at the same tiered rate for their category of business; it is not something one licensed casino does and another skips. It is not a factor that should push you towards one UKGC-licensed operator over another. What it does confirm is one more reason to stay within Gambling Commission-licensed sites: unlicensed operators, including ones that accept UK players without a UKGC licence, pay nothing into this system and fund none of the research, prevention or treatment it supports.
