UK-licensed online slots now have a legal ceiling on how much you can stake on a single spin. It is a small change in mechanics with a specific purpose, and it does not apply everywhere you might assume. Here is what it actually covers.

## What the limit is

Since it came into force, the maximum stake per spin on an online slot game offered by a UK-licensed operator is £5 for players aged 25 and over, and £2 for players aged 18 to 24. This is a hard cap enforced in the game itself, not a suggested limit or something you can request an exception to: the software will not accept a spin above the cap regardless of your balance, deposit history or VIP status with the operator.

## Why the age split

The lower £2 limit for 18-24 year olds reflects the Gambling Commission's own research showing that age group has a higher rate of gambling harm than older players, at the same stake sizes. It is applied by age, not by account tenure or spending history, so a 23-year-old with years of measured play is still capped at £2, and a newly-turned-25-year-old moves to £5 automatically.

## What counts as "online slots" here

The limit applies specifically to online slot games — the reel-based games sometimes called video slots or fruit machines online. It does not apply to other casino games such as roulette, blackjack or live dealer games, and it does not apply to sports betting or poker, which have their own separate stake and product rules. If you play a mix of slots and table games, only the slots carry this specific per-spin cap.

## What it does not limit

The cap is on stake per spin, not on how much you can lose in a session, how many spins you can play per minute, or how much you can deposit. A player at the £5 cap who plays continuously can still lose far more than £5 in an hour; the limit slows the ceiling on any single spin, it does not cap overall spend. Deposit limits, loss limits and time-based tools are separate features, usually set voluntarily by the player through the operator's account settings, not imposed by this rule.

## How it is enforced

Because the cap is built into the licence conditions for the games themselves, it applies at every UKGC-licensed operator identically — there is no licensed casino where you can pay for a higher per-spin stake on slots. If a site offers online slots with spins above these caps to UK players, that is a signal the operator is not applying UK licence conditions correctly, whether or not it holds a UKGC licence for other products.

## Why this matters when choosing where you play

An unlicensed site that accepts UK players without holding a UKGC licence is not bound by this rule at all, so higher per-spin slot stakes advertised to UK players are one of the more concrete tells that an operator sits outside UK regulation, alongside the absence of GAMSTOP integration and UKGC licence numbers you can check on the public register.
